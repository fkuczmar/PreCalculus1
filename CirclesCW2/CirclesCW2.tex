\begin{question}  \label{QKdf49edr4333}
\begin{enumerate}

\item Drag point $R$ in the worksheet below to approximate the coordinates of the point on the circle of radius $5$ centered at the origin that is closest to the point $B$ with coordinates $(-2,3)$. Then mark that position by dragging point $P$ there.

\begin{onlineOnly}
    \begin{center}
\desmos{6yotul255q}{900}{600}
\end{center}
\end{onlineOnly}

\href{https://www.desmos.com/calculator/6yotul255q}{141: Circles Tangent to a Given Crircle}

\item Drag point $R$ to approximate the coordinates of the point on the circle that is farthest from $B$. Then mark that position by dragging point $Q$ there.

\item Use algebra, not geometry or vectors, to find the exact coordinates of the points in parts (a) and (b). Do \emph{not} use a calculator. Explain your reasoning.

\item Enter the exact coordinates of these points in Lines 19 and 20 in the above worksheet.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{explanation}
The key idea is that the points on the circle closest to and farthest from point $B$ lie on the line through $B$ and the center of the circle. To find the coordinates of these closest and farthest points with algebra, we need to first find equations for the circle and the line.

A point $P$ lies on the circle centered at the origin with radius $5$ if and only if $P$ is exaclty 5 units from the origin. So if $P$ has coordinates $(x,y)$, the distance from $P$ to the origin is $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ and $P$ lies on the circle if and only if
\[
     \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = 5.
\]

To find an equation of the line through $B$ and the origin, we first find its slope. The slope is the rate of change of $y$ with respect to $x$ as we move along the line and is equal to 
\[
  \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{3 - 0}{-2 - 0} = - \frac{3}{2}.
\]
Now the point $P$ (other than the origin) with coordinates $(x,y)$ lies on the line through the origin with slope $-3/2$ if and only if the line through the origin and $P$ has slope $-3/2$. Since the line through $(0,0)$ and $(x,y)$ has slope
\[
 \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{y - 0}{x - 0} = \frac{y}{x},
\]
the point $(x,y)\neq (0,0$ lies on the line if and only if
\[
    \frac{y}{x} = -\frac{3}{2},
\]
or if and only if 
\[
 y = -\frac{3}{2}x.
\]

So the coordinates of the points where the line intersects the circle are the solutions to the system
\[
  \begin{cases}
  & \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = 5 \\
  & y = -\frac{3}{2}x .
\end{cases}
\]

To solve this system, substitute the expression for $y$ in the first equation into the second, then square both sides to get
\[
   \left( -\frac{3}{2}x \right)^2 + y^2 = 25.
\]
Then
\[
  \frac{13}{4}x^2 = 25 ,
\]
so
\[
    x^2 = \frac{100}{13},
\]
and 
\[
  x = \pm \frac{10}{\sqrt{13}}.
\]
If $x=\frac{10}{\sqrt{13}}$, then
\[
    y = -\frac{3}{2}x = -\frac{3}{2} \left(\frac{10}{\sqrt{13}}  \right) = -\frac{15}{\sqrt{13}}
\]
and if $x=-\frac{10}{\sqrt{13}}$,
\[
   y = \frac{15}{\sqrt{13}}.
\]

So the line through the origin and the point $B$ intersects the circle of radius $5$ centered at the origin in the points with coordinates,
\[
 (x,y) = \left(\frac{10}{\sqrt{13}} , -\frac{15}{\sqrt{13}}  \right) 
\]
and
\[
  (x,y) = \left(-\frac{10}{\sqrt{13}} , \frac{15}{\sqrt{13}}  \right).
\]
Looking at the graph, the first of these points is the point on the circle farthest from $B$, the second the point on the circle nearest $B$.
\end{explanation}

\end{question}
