\begin{question} \label{Qppef3231}
For prices betweeen $\$4$/burger and $\$12$/burger, the equation
\[
     25p + 4q = 495
\]
relates the price $p$ (measured in dollars/burger) to the average number of burgers sold/day at Five Guys in Shoreline.

\begin{enumerate}

\item What happens to the average number of burgers sold/day if Five Guys increases the price by $\$0.50$/burger? 

First work the question in general by writing an equation that relates a change in price of $\Delta p$ dollars/burger and the corresponding change $\Delta q$ in the average number of burgers sold/day. Then answer the specific question at hand.

\item How could they increase the average number of burgers they sell by $12$ burgers/day? 

\item At the start of business on Monday, Five Guys increases prices by $20\%$ . The result is a  $25\%$ decrease in the average in the average number of burgers sold/day. What was the price on Sunday, before the price increase? Use algebra to answer this question. Then check your answer is correct by going back to the original words of the problem, \emph{not} your equations.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{explanation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See the Explanation to Question 10 in the chapter \href{https://ximera.osu.edu/precalc1/PreCalculus1/LinearFunctions/LinearFunctions}{Linear Functions} of our class notes.

\item See the Explanation to Question 10 in the chapter \href{https://ximera.osu.edu/precalc1/PreCalculus1/LinearFunctions/LinearFunctions}{Linear Functions} of our class notes.

\item Let $p$ be the price (in dollars/burger) and $q$ the average number of burgers sold/day before the price increase. Then the change in price
\[
   \Delta p = 20\%(p) = \frac{1}{5}p
\] 
causes a change in the average number of burgers sold/day of
\[
    \Delta q = (-25\%)q = -\frac{1}{4}q.
\] 

From part (a) we know
\[
 25 \Delta p  + 4 \Delta q = 0.
\]
So
\[
 25 \left(  \frac{1}{5}p \right) - 4 \left( \frac{1}{4}q \right) = 0,
\]
\[
    5p - q = 0,
\]
and 
\[
  q  =5p.
\]
Substitute this expression for $p$ into the equation
\[
      25p + 4q = 495,
\]
relating $p$ and $q$ gives
\[
     25p + 4 (5p) = 495.
\]
Then 
\[
    45p =495
\]
and 
\[
  p = 11.
\]

Our conclusion is that Five Guys charged $\$11$/burger before the price increase and that increasing this price by $20\%$ decreases the average demand by $25\%$. I'll leave it for you to check this.

Here's another method that does not rely on the solution to part (a) directly.

After the increase, the new price is
\[
     p + \Delta p = p +\frac{1}{5}p =  \frac{6}{5}p
\]
and the average number of burgers sold/day is
\[
   q + \Delta q = q - \frac{1}{4}q =  \frac{3}{4}q.
\] 
So
\[
  25 \left(  \frac{6}{5}p \right)  + 4 \left( \frac{3}{4}q \right) = 495
\]
and
\[
     30p + 3q = 495.
\]
Since also
\[
      25p + 4q = 495,
\]
we have
\[
  30p + 3q = 25p + 4q,
\]
and
\[
    5p = q.
\]
Then continue as in the first solution.
\end{enumerate}
\end{explanation}

\end{question}

\begin{question} \label{QKdfder33}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Suppose the function $y=f(x)$ is linear with contant rate of change $\Delta y / \Delta x$. Then for any number $a$ in the domain of $f$, 
\begin{align*}
  f(x) &= f(a) + (f(x) - \answer{f(a)})  \\
        &= f(a) + \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} \left( \answer{x-a} \right)
\end{align*}

\emph{This is the point-slope form of an expression for a linear function.}

\item The function $y=f(x)$ is linear, with $f(4)=-3$ and $f(-1)=-1$. 
\begin{enumerate}
\item Sketch a rough graph of the function $y=f(x)$.
\item Use point-slope to find an expression for $f(x)$.
\item Solve the inequality $f(x) < 42$.
\item Solve the inequality $f(x) \geq f(74)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{question}
