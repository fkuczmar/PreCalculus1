\begin{question}\label{Qpf3m390}
Use the Pythagorean Theorem to find the exact distance between the points $A$ and $B$ with repsective coordinates $(3,-1)$ and $(-2,2)$. Start by plotting the points on a coordinate system. Then draw three segments to form a right triangle.

\begin{explanation}
Plot a third point, call it $C$, with coordinates $(3,2)$. Then $\Delta ABC$ is a right triangle with right angle at $C$. The two legs have lengths
\[
     a = BC = |3 - (-2)| = 5
\]
and
\[
     b = AC = | -1 - 2| = 3. 
\]
So by the Pythagorean Theorem the length of the hypotenuse of $\Delta ABC$ is
\[
 c = AB = \sqrt{5^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{34}.
\]
This is the distance between points $A$ and $B$.

Common errors:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Treating $A$ and $B$ as lengths. These are just \emph{names} of points, not lengths. They should not be part of  your computations.

\item Writing $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ without defining the meanings of $a$, $b$, and $c$.

\item Omitting to include the coordinates of the third point $C$.

\item Missing explanations about how to compute the lengths of the two legs.

\end{enumerate}



\end{explanation}
\end{question} \label{Qkf31222}


\begin{question} \label{Qodf3mr333}
Let ${\cal C}$ be the circle with center $A(3,1)$ and radius $7$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Use the Pythagorean Theorem to determine if the point $B(9,-2)$ inside, on, or outside ${\cal C}$. Start by drawing an accurate picture on a coordinate system.

\item Complete the following sentence.

\pskip

A point $P$ lies on the circle with center $A(3,1)$ and radius $7$ if and only if ...

\pskip

\item Use your description of the circle in part (b) to find an equation of that circle.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{explanation}
See the solutions to Examples 1 and 2 in the chapter \href{https://ximera.osu.edu/precalc1/PreCalculus1/Circles/Circles}{Circles, Part 1} of our class notes.

Common errors:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Most groups had incomplete explanations for parts (b) and (c). The idea is that a point $P$ lies on the circle with center $A(3,1)$ and radius $7$ if and only if the distance between $P$ and $A$ is equal to the radius ($7$). 

To find an equation of the circle, let $P$ have coordinates $(x,y)$ and continue as in Example 2 of the chapter above. In particular, if the vertex $C$ of right triangle $ACP$ has coordinates $(x,1)$, then leg $\overline{AC}$ has length
\[
   AC = | x-3 |.
\]
Be sure to include the absolute value so that the length is positive. Then since
\[
      |x-3|^2 = (x-3)^2,
\]
we can omit the absolute value when we square the length. 

Also, leave the equation of the circle as
\[
    \sqrt{(x-3)^2 + (y-1)^2} = 7.
\] 
Do \emph{not} square both sides as doing would obscure the meaning of the circle as the set of points exactly 7 units from its center. 
\end{enumerate}

\end{explanation}
\end{question}
